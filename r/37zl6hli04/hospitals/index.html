Stabilize and Transfer

Federal law says any emergency room shall stabilize whoever arrives, and may then transfer them. Almost everything else about a Lake County hospital, from the services it keeps to the owner it has to whether it stays open, is a may, and each one is held by someone you can name.

Status as of 9 November 2026

The starting point is a July 2026 Mother Jones investigation of the for-profit chain that owns Vista Medical Center East and of a patient who died there in January 2025. That story follows one owner. This chapter puts four questions to the whole county system. Who holds the decision? Is it a shall or a may? Who keeps the count? Who is left with the remainder when the holder does nothing?

One

One shall, many mays

The shall is short. Under the federal emergency-treatment law, known as EMTALA, a hospital with an emergency room has to examine and stabilize whoever arrives, and it may then transfer them. The duty attaches to the patient at the door. It does not attach to a trauma surgeon on call, an anesthesia contract, a maternity ward or the building.

Those are mays. The ledger below lists who holds each one, whether it binds or permits, who keeps the count, and what is left over. Everything in it comes from the documentary record compiled on 9 November 2026.

Three

Distance to trauma care for shootings

The gun chapter counted shootings in Lake County between January 2014 and March 2018 from the Gun Violence Archive: 74 shootings in which someone was hit, 97 people shot, suicides excluded. For each we measured the straight-line distance to the nearest Level I or II trauma center in the federal HIFLD file, once with Vista on the list and once without it.

Vista was the nearest center for 49 of the 74 shootings (66%) and for 66 of the 97 people shot (68%). For those 49, the median distance rises from 3.0 miles to 8.0. Without Vista the next nearest is Northwestern Lake Forest for 36 of them and Condell for 13. Condell, the county's only Level I center, is a median 9.1 miles from all 74 shootings. The gun chapter has the shootings themselves.

Four

One hospital, four owners

The Waukegan hospital opened in 1923 as Victory Memorial. It has been handed from a community nonprofit to Community Health Systems in 2006, to Quorum Health in 2016 and to American Healthcare Systems in 2023. The 2020 change of control and the 2023 sale both went through the state review board as exemptions, which "shall be approved" once the paperwork is complete.

DateWhat happenedFigure

Losses began under the previous owner

Medicare cost reports are the main public financial record of Vista in the files we hold. They are filed by the hospital and are not audited accounts. The fiscal year ends 30 November. Net income was positive through fiscal 2020 and negative from 2021, under Quorum. The sale to AHS closed on 1 July 2023, inside fiscal 2023, so that year spans both owners. Current liabilities went from $10.1M in 2018 to $118.0M in 2023, and the number of full-time-equivalent employees fell from 808 to 613.

Two cautions. Fiscal 2020 net income includes $17.6M of other income, and operating income that year was a loss of $5.8M. Fiscal 2019 current liabilities appear in the file as a negative $2.0M, which looks like a reporting quirk, and we show it as filed.

What AHS says

  • It inherited the problems when it bought the hospital in 2023, and says the state is holding back money to recover old debts.
  • The hospital will not close. AHS's lawyer says the hospital itself asked the state to suspend some services in October 2026.
  • At the October town hall its side said 20% of patients are uninsured and the hospital employs more than 600 people.
  • The owner's wife, Evelina Sarian, called the loss of trauma status a minor setback and said the family paid $25M. NBC reported about $15M of debt taken on and Becker's reported $15M spent on equipment, so the record disagrees on the figure.

The cost reports bear out part of this. The loss years began before the sale. They do not settle how much of the later debt AHS added.

Five

Where the patients went

Emergency visits at Vista fell 36.8% from 2018-19 to 2024-25, and visits at Lake Forest rose 30.1% (both derived). The state report card's 2022-23 emergency figures are missing from our source, and Condell has only the latest year. The years run October to September.

Delivery discharges tell a similar story. Highland Park's rose from 996 to 1,379 while Vista's fell from 1,186 to 354 and Good Shepherd's from 951 to 702. Each figure is our sum of the three published delivery groups (vaginal birth and two caesarean groups), so it is derived and understates total births. Official births by hospital were not found. Vista's 2023-24 figure of 228 falls well before the unit closed on 28 October 2025, and we could not explain the drop.

Six

Who pays and how it is rated

The measures differ, so here is what each one is. Medicaid, charity care and self-pay are shares of inpatients in 2024, as shown on the Illinois hospital report card. Would recommend is the share of surveyed patients who say they would. CMS stars are the federal overall rating, 1 to 5, which combines mortality, safety, readmission, patient-experience and timely-care measures (2026 release).

Vista carries the heaviest load of Medicaid (28.8% of inpatients, and 39.7% of outpatients) and the most charity care (12.9%), and it has the lowest recommendation score. The nonprofit systems differ among themselves. Highland Park has a Medicaid share of 6.7%, and Condell publishes charity care of 0.0%.

Seven

Money owed and money invested

These are different kinds of money, so they sit in two panels with their own scales. The first lists what Vista owes that has reached the public record, in court filings, press reports of court filings and state statements. The second lists capital that others announced or filed for county hospitals. A debt is a claim on what exists. A capital cost is a plan, and it says nothing about what was spent.

Eight

The coverage cliff

Lake County Medicaid enrollment fell from 168,616 in fiscal 2023 to 126,877 in fiscal 2026. That is 24.8% below the peak and 17.2% below fiscal 2022 (both derived), and it equals 17.6% of the county's population (derived, using the 2025 estimate of 719,339). Counts are taken on 30 June each year. Most of the fall is in the two adult groups that cover working-age people.

Enrollment is concentrated. The five ZIPs for Waukegan, North Chicago and Zion (including 60088, which is not charted) hold about 60,150 enrollees, roughly 44% of the county total (derived).

Method

Methods, sources and limits

Chart data are built by a script from the files named below into one JSON file, and every figure has a table twin under it. Derived figures are marked as derived. Nothing here comes from a web search run for this edition. The text rests on two research notes compiled on 9 November 2026 (a Vista record and a coverage-and-access record), each of which links the pages it read.

Limits

  • Distances are straight-line, not drive time, and the shootings are from 2014 to 2018.
  • Map layers use 2010 census tracts. Share uninsured and poverty are from the 2018 Social Vulnerability Index. Life expectancy is 2010 to 2015. The ZIP figures use 2017 to 2021 uninsured estimates. These vintages differ and we show them side by side only because nothing newer fit the same geometry.
  • Several Vista facts reached us through secondary reporting. News-Sun and Tribune stories came through Becker's, PressReader or aggregators, and the Mother Jones figures are its own reading of court records. The research notes say exact numbers should be checked against the linked pages.
  • The revocation date is 23 October in most reports and 24 October in one. The year of ComEd's default judgment is uncertain. The 2023 sale approval letter was not seen.
  • Delivery counts and ZIP Medicaid totals are our sums. The 2022-23 emergency figures are missing. Trauma levels for hospitals other than Vista are the 2021 federal list.
  • City of Hope's Zion hospital is still listed by CMS as proprietary under its old name, although it was sold to the nonprofit City of Hope on 2 February 2022. Cost-report income for it is unreliable and is not used. Advocate's balance-sheet fields for 2018 to 2022 are system-wide and are not used.
